Our Mission

First Chirp Farm is a living classroom and sanctuary rooted in the principles of permaculture and regenerative agriculture. We believe healthy land, thriving animals, and informed communities are inseparable.

Named for the hopeful first sounds of new life each morning — the chirps of birds and the soft calls of animals finding safety — we work to restore ecosystems while providing compassionate care to animals in need whenever we can.

Whether through growing nutrient-dense food, healing degraded soil, offering temporary or permanent homes for animals, or teaching others how to farm regeneratively, every action is guided by care for the whole living system.

Soil is life

Regenerative practices rebuild the foundation of everything we grow and care for.

The Farm

Working with nature, not against it

Permaculture Design

Zones, guilds, stacking functions, and closed-loop systems. We design landscapes that produce abundance while requiring less external input over time.

Soil Regeneration

Cover crops, composting, no-till methods, and diverse plantings rebuild organic matter, microbial life, and water-holding capacity year after year.

Integrated Animals

Chickens, goats, and other animals are partners in the system — fertilizing, pest control, and grazing in ways that enhance rather than deplete the land.

A Working, Teaching Landscape

Every bed, tree, pond, and pathway is intentional. Visitors and students experience how food production, wildlife habitat, water management, and animal welfare can support one another. We are continually learning and adapting as the land teaches us.

Learn with us

Helping Animals in Need

When capacity and resources allow, First Chirp Farm provides a safe, species-appropriate home for animals who need one. Whether temporary fostering, long-term sanctuary, or simply a peaceful place to recover, we treat every animal as an individual.

Our resident animals are integral members of the farm community. They contribute to the land through their natural behaviors while receiving excellent nutrition, veterinary care, and daily attention.

  • Species-appropriate housing and enrichment
  • Focus on quality of life over quantity
  • Transparent about our current capacity
